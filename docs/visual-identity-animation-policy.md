# World Makers Visual Identity, Image, Design & Animation Policy

**Policy ID:** `WM-VISUAL-001`  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Normative visual-direction policy  
**Scope:** game art, characters, environments, props, UI, VFX, animation, cinematics, screenshots, key art and marketing images.

## 1. Purpose

World Makers must be immediately recognizable as an original educational eco-fantasy universe. The visual system combines **nature, science, creation, learning and community** with a warm, optimistic, premium stylized-3D finish. Every visual decision must support play readability, child accessibility, curiosity and experimentation before decorative complexity.

The approved reference set lives in `docs/visual-reference/world-makers-v1/`. Those images are **directional references**, not runtime assets and not a license to reproduce any third-party style, mesh, composition or character. Production assets must remain original, source-controlled and traceable.

## 2. Brand promise translated into visuals

World Makers should communicate the following before the player reads any text:

- **Explore:** open horizons, readable landmarks, paths, bridges and layered elevation.
- **Learn:** tools, notebooks, scanners, laboratories and visual cause/effect feedback.
- **Create:** modular construction pieces that combine cleanly without becoming a cube-grid aesthetic.
- **Care:** living ecosystems, restoration, water, plants, biodiversity and visible environmental consequences.
- **Transform:** a brighter world emerges from player action; progress is shown as ecological and social improvement, not visual excess.

The emotional baseline is **curious, hopeful, capable and collaborative**. Avoid cynicism, grimdark framing, militarization and threat-heavy UI as default identity.

## 3. Canonical palette system

### 3.1 Brand and interface palette

| Token | Hex | Primary role |
|---|---|---|
| Exploration Blue | `#2F80ED` | navigation, exploration, active focus |
| Deep Blue | `#123B7A` | headings, deep UI surfaces, confidence |
| Bright Cyan | `#56CCF2` | technology, discovery, holographic accents |
| Life Green | `#27AE60` | ecology, restoration, healthy living systems |
| Growth Green | `#6FCF97` | secondary natural states, positive growth |
| Discovery Gold | `#F2C94C` | achievements, knowledge, discovery |
| Solar Orange | `#F2994A` | creativity, energy, construction emphasis |
| Warm Sand | `#E9D8A6` | terrain neutrals, warmth, material balance |
| Light White | `#F8FBFF` | clarity, cards, negative space |
| Mist Gray | `#DDE7F0` | neutral panels, separators, atmospheric support |

These values are identity anchors, not a mandate to flatten every biome into the same colors. The existing Caribbean Rainforest biome palette in `docs/art-style-guide.md` remains valid as a world palette. **Brand/UI tokens and biome/material tokens are separate layers.**

### 3.2 Color rules

- Color must reinforce meaning but never be the only carrier of state.
- Success/valid placement must also use iconography, shape or stencil identity; failure/invalid placement must do the same.
- Saturation should concentrate around points of interaction and discovery, while large environmental surfaces remain controlled enough to preserve hierarchy.
- Avoid persistent neon saturation, pure-black UI blocks, muddy brown scenes and rainbow-everywhere compositions.
- Accessibility contrast and representative-device testing override aesthetic preference.

## 4. Shape language and originality boundary

### 4.1 Required shape language

The World Makers silhouette vocabulary combines:

- rounded rectangles and softened geometric modules;
- cylinders, tapered prisms and curved structural transitions;
- arches and bridges as connection motifs;
- domes, observatories and greenhouse-like forms;
- terraces, planters and biophilic modules;
- stylized natural rock, soil and vegetation shapes;
- clean icon silhouettes based on leaves, atoms, tools, books, water and community.

Geometry may be modular without looking mass-produced. Repetition must be broken by proportion, rotation, ecological overgrowth, material variation and silhouette rhythm.

### 4.2 Prohibited visual shortcuts

World Makers must **not** use the following as its defining visual language:

- terrain made primarily from identical cubes or a visible voxel/block grid;
- pixel-art surface textures on 3D world geometry;
- dirt/grass cube stacks that read as a clone of voxel-survival games;
- copied silhouettes, UI frames, character proportions, icons, creature designs or material patterns from identifiable commercial games;
- marketplace assets used unchanged as hero identity;
- overly realistic military, industrial or dystopian design language.

This is an originality rule, not a ban on geometric construction. A cube can exist as a primitive or object when functionally justified; a **cube-grid world aesthetic cannot become the brand**.

## 5. Environment and world design

### 5.1 World character

The world is a bright stylized eco-fantasy setting in which science and nature coexist. Signature world features may include waterfalls, high plateaus, suspended or elevated structures, observatories, ecological laboratories, bridges, wind and solar infrastructure, restoration zones and monumental natural landmarks.

### 5.2 Composition rules

- Preserve a readable play corridor and at least one strong landmark per scene.
- Use quiet construction zones framed by denser vegetation or geology.
- Build depth in three layers: foreground framing, playable midground and aspirational background landmark.
- Use verticality to invite exploration, but maintain navigational clarity.
- Water, cliffs and foliage must frame interaction rather than obscure it.
- Environmental storytelling should show consequences: healthier water, plant growth, restored habitats, energy flow or community development.

### 5.3 Terrain language

Terrain is **organic-modular**, not voxel. Use stepped terraces only when softened by irregular rock faces, soil transitions, vegetation, erosion, curved paths or authored edges. Repeated 90-degree block silhouettes must not dominate the horizon.

## 6. Architecture and construction pieces

Construction should feel learnable and modular while retaining authored design quality.

Preferred families:

- arches and modular bridges;
- domes and greenhouse modules;
- research stations and observation towers;
- planters, water-treatment modules and renewable-energy structures;
- platforms with rounded or chamfered profiles;
- connectors that visibly explain how pieces relate.

Every build kit needs a coherent silhouette family, connector logic, palette assignment, LOD plan and tablet budget. Construction pieces should snap predictably, but the visual result must avoid looking like a stack of identical blocks.

## 7. Character policy

### 7.1 Character identity

World Makers characters are **stylized child/young-explorer avatars**: expressive, capable and approachable rather than miniature adults. The existing 4.5–5-head family and 5-head `ChildExplorerV1` production target remain authoritative.

Core character traits:

- enlarged head, hands and footwear for readability;
- large, clear eyes and brows capable of communicating intent at tablet distance;
- simplified but believable anatomy;
- practical explorer clothing with layered textiles, utility belts, patches and modular accessories;
- backpacks, notebooks, scanners and scientific tools as recurring functional motifs;
- hair and clothing with strong silhouette separation;
- diversity expressed through skin tone, hair, facial features, clothing and accessories without assigning a default gender silhouette.

### 7.2 Character silhouette families

The approved reference set establishes a family, not fixed immutable cast models. Production characters may include archetypes such as curious explorer, scientific inventor, nature guardian and knowledge explorer. Each must remain distinguishable in monochrome silhouette before color and accessories are considered.

### 7.3 Faces and expression

Minimum expression vocabulary for principal characters:

- neutral/attentive;
- joy/achievement;
- curiosity/questioning;
- surprise/discovery;
- determination/focus;
- calm/empathy.

Expressions must remain readable without uncanny realism. Avoid exaggerated fear, violence-coded aggression or manipulative “reward face” loops as default feedback.

## 8. Props, tools and technology

Tools should look **friendly, purposeful and understandable**. Use rounded housings, protected edges, compact proportions and clear semantic indicators. Scientific devices may use cyan/blue light accents; ecological tools may use green accents; creation/building systems may use orange/gold accents.

A prop must visually explain at least one of these questions: what does it measure, what does it change, what does it store or how does the player hold/use it?

Avoid generic sci-fi guns, weapon-like scanners and overly tactical accessories unless a non-violent functional distinction is unmistakable.

## 9. UI and information design

The UI should feel like an educational field instrument rather than a dense admin dashboard.

- Rounded card geometry and generous internal padding.
- Blue/cyan translucent or light surfaces may be used, but text contrast must remain robust.
- Icons must work independently of color and be recognizable at small size.
- Use one dominant action per panel whenever possible.
- Minimize persistent HUD coverage; reveal contextual controls near the moment of need.
- Keep mission checklists short and visually scannable.
- Use nature/science/building icon families consistently.
- Avoid ornamental frames, faux-pixel UI, aggressive red warning chrome and excessive floating widgets.

### 9.1 Motion in UI

Recommended timing bands:

- micro feedback: **120–180 ms**;
- panel/state transition: **180–280 ms**;
- large reveal: **300–450 ms**, only when it does not interrupt control.

Use ease-out for arrival, ease-in for dismissal and smooth ease-in-out for camera-linked presentation. Reduced-motion mode must preserve all meaning with no required translation, shake or scale pulse.

## 10. Animation policy

### 10.1 Animation character

Animation is **fluid, expressive and physically legible**, not hyper-realistic. It should emphasize intention and cause/effect.

Required principles:

- anticipation before major actions;
- clear weight transfer;
- readable arcs for hands, tools and body center;
- follow-through and overlap on hair, backpack straps and loose accessories;
- restrained squash/stretch on body and props where it enhances clarity without rubberizing the world;
- pose clarity before high-frequency secondary motion;
- short settle phases after jumps, placement actions and discoveries.

The current 30 fps authored animation source contract remains compatible with this policy. Root motion stays disabled by default unless an explicit gameplay contract says otherwise.

### 10.2 Locomotion

Idle, start, walk, run, stop, turn, jump, fall and land must feel part of one motion family. Running should communicate curiosity and momentum rather than combat urgency. Landing must show believable absorption without punishing delay.

### 10.3 Interaction animation

Build, measure, observe, inspect, pickup and science-manipulate animations must prioritize hand/tool alignment and clear action staging. The player should be able to infer the action even with UI hidden.

### 10.4 Facial animation

Use a small, high-quality expression set driven by brows, lids, mouth corners, head tilt and eye focus. Avoid continuous exaggerated facial motion. Facial animation must not depend on biometric capture or child facial datasets.

### 10.5 Environmental animation

Wind, leaves, water, energy indicators, plant growth and restoration effects should make the world feel alive, but should not create constant full-screen motion. Motion density must fall in construction and reading zones.

### 10.6 VFX

VFX should visualize mechanics, not obscure them. Prefer clean particles, ribbons, soft emissive accents, growth traces, water/energy flow and concise reveal effects. Avoid persistent screen-filling bloom, heavy smoke, strobing, dense sparks or combat-coded impact language.

## 11. Camera and presentation

Gameplay presentation must preserve player agency. The repository's existing V7 camera contract remains authoritative for camera distances, FOVs and reduced-motion bounds.

Visual direction adds these rules:

- exploration views should expose landmarks and horizon depth;
- build views should prioritize site readability and piece relationship;
- science/observe views should frame tools and subject matter without becoming cutscenes;
- reveals should be short, player-controlled microbeats rather than long scripted cinematics;
- camera shake is never required for comprehension;
- first-person views must keep held tools and reticles visually minimal and avoid camera clipping or exaggerated bob.

## 12. Lighting and materials

Use a premium stylized-PBR approach:

- broad readable roughness groups rather than noisy microdetail;
- warm natural key light balanced by cool sky/environment fill;
- selective emissive accents for technology and discovery;
- vegetation and water should read clearly without expensive translucency as baseline identity;
- avoid universal plastic gloss, extreme bloom, crushed shadows and photoreal texture noise.

The tablet-first material and texture budgets in `docs/art-style-guide.md` remain binding.

## 13. Marketing image and screenshot policy

Key art and screenshots must represent the actual visual promise of the game without implying features that do not exist.

Required:

- original World Makers characters and world assets;
- readable focal point and clear environmental story;
- no third-party IP, trademarks or copied layouts;
- UI shown only when it demonstrates real gameplay;
- no fake monetization, loot, weapons or competitive cues inconsistent with the product;
- compositions should express discovery, construction, experimentation or restoration.

Concept art may be more aspirational than runtime screenshots, but it must be labeled as concept/reference when fidelity exceeds current production state.

## 14. Accessibility, child safety and comfort

- Provide reduced-motion equivalents for presentation and UI motion.
- Do not require rapid flashing or strobing; avoid repeated high-contrast flashes.
- Preserve readable text, icon size and controller/touch targets on tablets.
- Keep critical feedback independent of hue alone.
- Avoid imagery designed to create fear of missing out, purchase pressure or manipulative reward urgency.
- Character customization must not require biometric data, facial scanning or voice cloning.

## 15. Production governance

### 15.1 Asset review gates

A visual asset is not production-ready until it passes:

1. **Originality gate:** silhouette, composition and surface treatment are original and do not imitate a recognizable third-party game.
2. **Identity gate:** conforms to World Makers palette, shape language and emotional tone.
3. **Gameplay-readability gate:** action and collision silhouette are understandable at target viewing distance.
4. **Accessibility gate:** contrast, state redundancy and motion comfort reviewed.
5. **Performance gate:** LOD, texture, materials, overdraw and animation cost tested against target tier.
6. **Provenance gate:** source, author/license and derivative rights are documented.
7. **Native review gate:** imported Unreal asset is reviewed before any `authoredPresent=true` claim.

### 15.2 Reference hierarchy

When references conflict, use this order:

1. child safety, privacy, accessibility and legal/IP requirements;
2. gameplay readability and device performance;
3. this visual identity policy;
4. `docs/art-style-guide.md` technical production rules;
5. concept/reference images;
6. individual artist preference.

## 16. Definition of done for new visual features

A visual feature is complete only when its PR includes the relevant source/provenance, target asset path, screenshots or capture evidence, budget statement, accessibility/reduced-motion note when applicable, and confirmation that it does not reintroduce the prohibited voxel/pixel-copy visual language.

---

**Identity statement:** World Makers is a living, optimistic, science-and-nature creation universe. It may use modular construction, but its world must read as **organic, authored and uniquely World Makers** rather than as a block-grid derivative of another sandbox game.
