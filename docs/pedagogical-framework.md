# Pedagogical Framework / Marco pedagógico

> Learning in World Makers emerges through meaningful play. The project does not assume that difficult ideas are inappropriate for children; it adapts representation, scaffolding and interaction while preserving conceptual truth.

## Core design principles

1. **High conceptual ceiling** — children may encounter advanced ideas when the world makes them manipulable, observable and meaningful.
2. **Low interaction friction** — simplify controls and explanations, not the underlying concept.
3. **Learning by doing** — objectives are expressed through building, observing, comparing, predicting, experimenting, communicating, interpreting, reasoning and revising.
4. **Layered epistemic depth** — missions can expose the same idea through experience, explicit concept naming and formalization.
5. **Visible evidence** — each mission defines observable player actions that count as evidence of understanding.
6. **Non-punitive iteration** — mistakes change outcomes, reveal information and invite revision rather than causing shame or destructive loss.
7. **Transfer** — concepts should reappear in new constructions, environments, stories and decisions.
8. **Cross-disciplinary play** — strong adventures combine disciplines when the world problem naturally requires them.
9. **No worksheet dependency** — a mission fails pedagogical review if its learning can be removed without materially changing gameplay.
10. **Privacy minimization** — evidence uses stable objective/event IDs and minimized records rather than sensitive child profiling.

## Depth model

### Experience
The player encounters the idea through action before formal terminology is required.

### Concept
The game names and explains the pattern the player has experienced.

### Formalization
The game can introduce formulas, diagrams, notation, technical vocabulary, structured argument or explicit textual analysis when useful.

Age bands control scaffolding, interface density, reading load and formal notation. They do not define a hard ceiling on which ideas a child may encounter.

## Core learning streams

### 1. Mathematics
Number sense, fractions, ratios, proportion, patterns, equations, estimation, optimization and coordinates.

Gameplay examples: allocate materials under constraints; discover a numerical rule in a changing structure; tune ratios to stabilize a machine.

### 2. Geometry
Angles, symmetry, transformations, tessellation, scale, area, volume, coordinate geometry, Pythagorean reasoning and spatial proof.

Gameplay examples: rebuild a floating city; align structures; design a dome; transform patterns; solve geometric routes.

### 3. English language
Contextual vocabulary, syntax, listening, speaking, reading comprehension, inference, functional writing and bilingual transfer.

Gameplay examples: negotiate with characters, decode instructions, reconstruct dialogue, write a message that changes a quest outcome.

### 4. Spanish language
Semantic precision, syntax, morphology, comprehension, inference, figurative language, oral expression and functional writing.

Gameplay examples: repair a living library, interpret ambiguous clues, choose precise wording, construct short texts with world consequences.

### 5. Literature
Plot, character, myth, legend, archetype, point of view, symbolism, theme, intertextuality and comparative interpretation.

World Makers prioritizes explorable adaptations of universal myths and legends with provenance and cultural review. Players should investigate stories, not merely answer questions about them.

### 6. Biology
Cells, organelles, tissues, organ systems, microorganisms, heredity, variation, adaptation, life cycles and homeostasis.

Gameplay examples: enter a living cell, restore a biological system, compare organisms, trace inheritance or adaptation through a population.

### 7. Chemistry
Matter, atoms, molecules, states, mixtures, solutions, solubility, acids/bases, reaction evidence, conservation of mass, reaction rate and energy change.

Gameplay examples: operate a virtual alchemical laboratory, separate mixtures, predict reaction evidence, balance material transformations and investigate environmental chemistry. Hazardous phenomena may be represented conceptually without providing actionable unsafe real-world procedures.

### 8. Physics
Forces, motion, energy, work, simple machines, vectors, momentum, buoyancy, electricity, circuits, waves, sound, optics, gravity and orbital reasoning.

Gameplay examples: repair an orbital forge, build machines that physically work, redirect light, tune resonance or stabilize a moving system.

### 9. Ecology
Ecosystems, interdependence, food webs, biodiversity, water/carbon/nutrient cycles, carrying capacity, succession, habitat fragmentation and stewardship trade-offs.

Gameplay examples: restore or redesign a persistent ecosystem and predict secondary consequences of intervention.

### 10. Ethics
Fairness, equality, need, consent, promises, responsibility, stewardship, consequences, common good and perspective-taking.

Ethical missions should not hide one ideological answer. Evidence should focus on reasons, consistency, attention to affected perspectives and willingness to revise.

### 11. Philosophy for children
Identity, knowledge, truth, causality, freedom, mind, language, assumptions, argument, contradiction, counterexample and reason revision.

Gameplay examples: rebuild a Ship of Theseus, investigate a Cave of Shadows, confront a prediction machine or reason through a world where nobody can lie.

## History and culture as cross-curricular context

History and culture remain essential context layers across literature, architecture, myths, technology, place and environmental design. Real traditions require provenance, respectful adaptation and appropriate expert/community review.

## Mission metadata minimum

Every production pedagogical mission should eventually define:

- stable mission ID;
- primary discipline and optional secondary disciplines;
- technical curriculum axis;
- target scaffold/age profile;
- concept tags;
- experience/concept/formalization depth layers;
- learning objective IDs;
- fantasy premise and player-facing goal;
- gameplay mechanic mapping;
- success/evidence events;
- misconceptions or alternate outcomes worth detecting;
- scaffolds/hints;
- difficulty;
- localization/read-aloud requirements;
- pedagogy and safety review status;
- cultural review status when applicable;
- analytics/privacy classification.
